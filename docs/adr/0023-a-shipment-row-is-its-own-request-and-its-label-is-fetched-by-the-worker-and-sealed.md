# A Shipment row is its own request, and its Label is fetched by the worker and sealed

Status: accepted (issue #126)

A person asks for a Shipment in the panel, but only the worker opens a Connection's credentials, and a Carrier such as InPost has no idempotency key: a create that is repeated blindly buys a second parcel. We decided the `shipment` row is the request. `requestShipment` inserts it as `requested` and due (`nextCheckAt`, swept by `sync.tick` like the pending status push of ADR 0012); the `shipments.create` job asks the Carrier with the row's id as `reference` and its `createdAt` as `requestedAt`, and stores the answer, so a job that died after the Carrier answered leaves a row that still asks, and its retry gets the same Shipment back from the connector, which must find it by that reference. A lease on the row (`createLeaseUntil`) keeps two runs from asking at once, and a run from asking too soon after one whose outcome is not known: the two cases a lookup cannot cover. The Label is fetched by `shipments.track` once the Carrier confirms the Shipment and stored in the row, sealed with `SecretBox` and bound to the organization and the Shipment, next to the destination a person confirmed, sealed the same way. Both are Buyer data (ADR 0016): the Label prints a name and an address, and a pickup point is usually a few streets from the Buyer's home.

## Considered options

- An outbox table of shipment requests: a second row to scope, deduplicate and clean up for what the Shipment already says about itself.
- Calling the Carrier from the panel's request: the web process would open credentials, which it never does, and a slow Carrier would hold the request.
- Fetching the Label when a person downloads it: the same, on every download.
- Storing a link to the Label: ShipX has no public one, and a link would put the Buyer's address outside the sealed data.
- Copying the address into the Shipment: a second sealed copy to erase; the Order's own is read when the Carrier is asked.

## Consequences

- A connector's `shipments.create` must be repeatable by `reference`; the core never calls it for a Shipment that has an `externalId` or left `requested`, and only with a request that names a service the connector declares and fits it (checked when the person asks and again in the job).
- **A create whose outcome is not known is not repeated for five minutes** (`SHIPMENT_CREATE_RETRY_DELAY_MS`, a number the SDK's contract states). A Carrier without an idempotency key makes a create repeatable by looking the earlier Shipment up, and InPost's list was seen to lag behind its own create (sandbox, 2026-10-10: a new Shipment showed 1.4 to 5.4 seconds after the `POST` was sent, over six samples), so a repeat that comes at once buys a second label; the queue's own retries come 2, 4, 8 and 16 seconds later. So the lease is not given back when a call throws or its answer cannot be stored: it is taken again from that moment, a job that comes earlier returns without a call and without counting an attempt, and the Shipment is due when the lease runs out, which is when the tick's sweep brings it back. Only a call that Hanza's own rate limiter refused before any request but a read was sent gives the lease back and is retried by the queue as usual; a Connection that cannot be opened fails before the lease is taken. Neither the 24 hour timeout nor a cancel without asking happens while a lease is in force. A consequence: a transient failure of a create marks the Connection failing at once, since no retry of that job asks again.
- An Order takes no second Shipment through the same Connection and service while one is still `requested` (`shipment_already_requested`): a double click, or two members, would each buy a label. Once the first is at the Carrier, cancelled or failed, another may be asked for.
- The tick claims overdue `requested` Shipments and enqueues one `shipments.create` each (one per tick while the Connection is failing), never one whose lease is in force. For Shipments the Carrier knows it only enqueues the Connection's `shipments.track`, and that job claims its own batch of at most 100: its payload names the Connection, not the Shipments, so a claim by the tick would leave it nothing to find. A batch whose call failed is given back at once when the queue or a sign-in will retry it soon; after a refusal for good or the last attempt it waits out the claim (10 minutes).
- Cancelling runs in `shipments.track` too, before the states of the same batch, so one job per Connection is the only writer of what a Carrier says about a Shipment it knows. A Shipment the Carrier was never successfully asked for, with no lease in force, is cancelled without a call. A cancel a person asks for while the Shipment's batch is being tracked leaves it due at once, and the job enqueues a run right after itself.
- What fails for one Shipment in `shipments.track` stays that Shipment's: a cancel or a Label the Carrier's API refuses is recorded on the Connection, the rest of the batch is tracked and applied (the Shipment whose cancel failed too), and the first such error is thrown when the batch is done. Otherwise one cancel that keeps failing would keep a pickup of another Shipment from ever shipping its Order. A sign-in that is needed, a rate limit, or a failure of the track call itself still stop the run.
- A Shipment never goes back: a final status is never left, one the Carrier had is never again `ready` or `pending`, and a confirmed one is never again `pending` (`statusStage`). A report of an earlier stage is logged and changes nothing but the next check; `handedOverAt` and the shipped Order are never undone. The 24 hour timeout applies only to a Shipment unconfirmed both in the row and in the report.
- The SDK cannot ask "does a Shipment with this reference exist" without creating one. So a Shipment cancelled locally, timed out, or still waiting after a create whose answer was lost may exist at the Carrier. The row says so (`createOutcomeUnknown`, shown by the panel as `mayExistAtCarrier`: a label may have been bought, check at the carrier) and so do the Events that end it (`createAttempted` on `shipment.failed` and on the cancelled `shipment.status_changed`). A Shipment the Carrier refused is not one. A lookup capability would close it.
- Checks follow the status: the first one six seconds after the Carrier took a create (`SHIPMENT_FIRST_CHECK_MS`, just after the longest lag seen in the Carrier's list, since a person waits for the Label); then every tick for the first 10 minutes while unconfirmed, then every 10 minutes; every 15 minutes when ready, but every tick during the first hour while its Label is still awaited, because a person is waiting to print it; hourly once the Carrier has the parcel. Unconfirmed after 24 hours it fails with `carrier_timeout`; the sweep does not visit a Connection that waits for sign-in, so a Shipment whose Connection waited that long fails at the first run after the sign-in, not at 24 hours; not final after 60 days it is no longer checked.
- A final status deletes the Label. An Erasure of the Order's Buyer data clears the Label and the destination of its Shipments, and a Label is neither fetched nor stored for an erased Order (the store takes the Order lock, which an Erasure waits for). Status, Carrier status and tracking number stay in plaintext.
- The panel reads Shipments without the sealed columns and learns that a Label exists from `labelContentType` (set and cleared with it, by a CHECK). A Label is served with a content type from an allow-list (PDF, PNG, printer commands as plain text, otherwise a download), whatever the connector called it.
- A sealed Label sits in a text column, at most 5 MB of file. A Label that is too large, is not a Label, or that the Carrier refuses for good is given up on: `labelFailureCode` (`too_large`, `invalid`, `refused`) is set, the Shipment id is logged, and the file is not asked for again at every check; a person prints it at the Carrier. A Carrier that has none yet (`TransientError`) is asked again. Nothing brings a Label that was given up on back.
- While a Shipment stays unconfirmed, a change of its Carrier's own status alone is an Event (`shipment.carrier_status_changed`): that code is then all that says what it waits for, such as funds on the account. Under a later status it is tracking detail, stored without an Event, so a poll never floods an Order's timeline.
- Every session of the database runs in UTC, pinned by `createDb` (`withUtcSession`). The times of this ADR and of ADR 0012 are read from SQL's `now()` and compared with it, the driver reads a `timestamptz` by dropping its offset, and every `DateTime` column is a `timestamp` holding UTC: in a session with another zone (the default of a server set up in local time), the request time sent to a Carrier was two hours late for Warsaw, its lookup of an earlier create began after the create, and the repeat bought a second parcel. A startup option beats the server's, the database's and the role's default; fixing one query with `AT TIME ZONE` would leave the others disagreeing with it.
- A `courier` Connection now has sync state (`shipments_create`, `shipments_track`) and health like a Channel's.
